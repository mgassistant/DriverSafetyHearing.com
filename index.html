Driver Safety hearing types

The issues are different. The preparation should be too.

California DMV Driver Safety hearings are administrative proceedings. The evidence, deadlines, and questions depend on the action being reviewed.

APS

Administrative Per Se

DUI-related suspension or revocation actions involving alcohol or chemical-test issues. APS hearings are separate from the criminal case.

PTS

Negligent Operator / NOTS

Point-count, collision, violation, and driving-history hearings used to decide whether negligent-operator action is appropriate.

MED

Medical Reexamination

Hearings involving medical conditions, driving ability, medical evidence, testing, probation, restriction, suspension, or revocation.

DS

Other Driver Safety Actions

Other administrative matters can involve financial responsibility, reexamination, or separate DMV actions affecting the driving privilege.

What a hearing can involve

You have the opportunity to respond to DMV’s case.

DMV’s administrative hearing process allows drivers to review the evidence, present relevant evidence and testimony, respond to evidence against them, and use witnesses when appropriate. A driver may also choose an attorney or another representative at their own expense.

Representation & advocacy

You do not necessarily have to handle the administrative process alone.

California DMV states that a driver may be represented by an attorney or another representative at the driver’s expense. Administrative representation is different from criminal-court representation and does not replace legal advice from an attorney.

For cases accepted for advocacy, an authorized representative may assist with Driver Safety communications, hearing scheduling, record/discovery review, evidence preparation, and appearance at the administrative hearing within the scope of the authorization.

California Driver Safety hearing resource

What to know before a DMV administrative hearing.

Driver Safety hearings are separate from court

California DMV explains that its administrative hearings are separate from criminal court proceedings. They are conducted before a Driver Safety Hearing Officer, and the evidentiary rules are less strict than those used in criminal court.

Hearing-request deadlines can be short

DMV’s general Driver Safety hearing guide states that a hearing typically must be requested within 10 days of receiving notice or 14 days from the date of a mailed notice. Some specific actions have their own rules; APS actions generally use a 10-day deadline from receipt of the suspension or revocation order.

Your hearing rights

DMV states that drivers can be represented by an attorney or another representative at their own expense, testify, review DMV evidence, present relevant evidence and witnesses, respond to DMV evidence, and appeal an adverse decision through available review processes.

NOTS and medical hearings are different from APS

NOTS hearings review the driving record, point count, collision and violation history, and whether negligent-operator action is appropriate. Medical Driver Safety proceedings may focus on medical evidence, reexamination, testing, driving ability, probation, restriction, suspension, or revocation.

How soon do I need to request a DMV hearing?

It depends on the action. DMV’s general administrative-hearing guide states that hearings generally must be requested within 10 days of receiving notice or 14 days from the date of a mailed notice. APS actions generally require a request within 10 days of receiving the order.

Can a non-attorney represent me at a DMV administrative hearing?

California DMV states that you may be represented by an attorney or another representative at your own expense. Non-attorney administrative representation is not the same as legal representation in criminal court.

Can I see DMV’s evidence before the hearing?

DMV describes a right to review its evidence, and in Driver Safety proceedings drivers can present relevant evidence and respond to the evidence against them. The process for obtaining documents depends on the case.

What happens at a NOTS hearing?

The hearing reviews the correctness of the driving record, point count, collisions, violations, driving history, and whether negligent-operator action against the driving privilege is appropriate.

What happens in a medical Driver Safety case?

DMV may review medical information and may require reexamination or testing. Depending on the evidence, DMV can take no action or impose probation, restrictions, suspension, or revocation.
